01 — FLAGSHIP HERO PROJECT · THERMAL TOPOLOGY OPTIMIZATION

DynaMesh-Thermal

GPU-Accelerated Thermal Topology Optimization
Verified C++/CUDA
FLOW: PHYSICS → DISCRETIZATION (FVM) → SOLVER (cuSPARSE) → GPU UNIFIED MEMORY → OPTIMIZATION
01 · The Engineering Problem
How can thermal-fluid dissipation be modeled while simultaneously optimizing material layouts?
High-density power electronics, heat exchangers, and battery cold plates require complex internal fluid channels to maximize thermal dissipation while minimizing pumping power. Classical topology optimization algorithms run out of memory or stall on CPU solvers because constructing dense global system matrices for coupled fluid-thermal equations is computationally prohibitive at fine spatial resolutions.
02 · What I Needed to Model
Navier–Stokes/Darcy Momentum & Advection-Diffusion Energy Transport
The governing continuum physics requires simultaneous solution of:
  • Fluid Momentum: Incompressible Navier–Stokes with Brinkman/Darcy friction term $\alpha(\gamma)\mathbf{u}$ penalizing fluid velocity in solid regions.
  • Incompressibility: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$, enforcing mass conservation via pressure Poisson solve.
  • Thermal Energy: Steady-state advection-diffusion transport $\rho C_p \mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla T - \nabla \cdot (k(\gamma) \nabla T) = Q$.
  • Material Interpolation: SIMP/RAMP penalization interpolating permeability $\alpha(\gamma)$ and thermal conductivity $k(\gamma)$ as functions of design density $\gamma \in [0,1]$.
03 · My Approach
Zero-Dense-Allocation Finite Volume GPU Architecture
Rather than relying on generic FEA software or allocating $O(N^2)$ global matrices in host memory, I architected a custom GPU solver in modern C++ and CUDA. The domain is discretized using a cell-centered Finite Volume Method (FVM), strictly guaranteeing mass and energy flux conservation across all internal cell interfaces. A continuous SIMP penalization steers intermediate densities toward binary (solid/fluid) structures.
04 · Implementation
C++, CUDA, cuSPARSE, CUB, and Unified Memory
The numerical engine is written in native C++ and CUDA:
  • Linear Systems: Built Compressed Sparse Row (CSR) coefficient representations on the fly and solved pressure Poisson equations via cuSPARSE conjugate gradient solvers on device.
  • Memory Hierarchy: Leveraged CUDA Unified Memory with cudaMemAdviseSetPreferredLocation to eliminate CPU-GPU round-trip PCIe transfer bottlenecks.
  • Reductions & Filtering: Used CUB device-wide reduction primitives for objective evaluation, sensitivity filtering, and mesh smoothing across 100k+ elements.
05 · Verification
Conservation Proofs & Benchmark Comparison
Verified the implementation against standard 2D benchmark heat-sink channels. Confirmed global energy balance $\sum Q_{\text{in}} = \sum Q_{\text{out}}$ with < 0.05% relative discretization error across cell boundaries. Verified that pressure drop and temperature contours match classical analytical Hagen-Poiseuille limits in straight-duct regimes before optimization initiates.
06 · Result
GPU Topology Optimization Pipeline Without Host RAM Constraints
Achieved complete GPU execution of the thermal optimization loop. The pipeline evaluates fluid velocity fields, solves temperature distribution, calculates adjoint sensitivities, and updates material layout without ever instantiating a dense matrix in system memory, yielding clean branched tree-like thermal conduits that outperform uniform channel baselines.
07 · What I Learned
Hardware Realities in Multiphysics Engineering
Writing a GPU solver from scratch taught me that physical equations cannot be divorced from memory architecture. Coalesced global memory access, divergence-free thread branches, and sparse representation dictate performance far more than raw FLOPS. True computational mechanics begins when you structure your physical discretization around the hardware that executes it.

02 — SOLID MECHANICS FLAGSHIP · CONTACT & WEAR MECHANICS

MicroWear-PINN

Micromechanics: Surface Observations → Wear Kinetics → Contact Mechanics → Elastic Stress Field
Verified Parity
MICROMECHANICS: SURFACE OBSERVATIONS → WEAR KINETICS (ARCHARD) → CONTACT MECHANICS → ELASTIC STRESS FIELD (AIRY) → PREDICTION
01 · The Engineering Problem
Subsurface Contact Stresses Cannot Be Measured Directly In Situ
In rolling-element bearings, dry clutches, and gear contacts, subsurface stresses cause subsurface fatigue and catastrophic spalling. However, optical and stylus profilometers can only measure the exterior wear scar at intermittent inspection intervals. Reconstructing the continuous internal elastic stress state from sparse surface observations is an ill-posed inverse problem in classical mechanics.
02 · What I Needed to Model
Archard Wear Kinetics Coupled with Biharmonic Airy Stress Potentials
The mechanical system must satisfy continuum elasticity and surface kinematics simultaneously:
  • Kinematic Surface Evolution: The Archard differential wear law: $\frac{\partial h(x,t)}{\partial t} = k_w \cdot p(x,t) \cdot v_{\text{rel}}$.
  • Elastic Equilibrium: Plane strain linear elasticity formulated through the Airy stress potential $\Phi(x,y)$, guaranteeing exact stress equilibrium ($\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma} = 0$) and compatibility via the biharmonic PDE: $\nabla^4 \Phi = \frac{\partial^4 \Phi}{\partial x^4} + 2\frac{\partial^4 \Phi}{\partial x^2 \partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^4 \Phi}{\partial y^4} = 0$.
  • Contact Boundary Conditions: Normal surface traction must match contact pressure: $\sigma_{yy}(x,0,t) = -p(x,t)$, with shear traction $\tau_{xy}(x,0,t) = \mu p(x,t)$.
03 · My Approach
Decoupled Dual-Network Physics-Informed Coordinate Representation
I designed a dual-network architecture that decouples surface kinematics from internal continuum elasticity. The first network maps spatial-temporal coordinates to wear scar depth $h(x,t)$, while the second network maps spatial domain coordinates $(x,y)$ to the Airy potential $\Phi(x,y)$. The biharmonic operator and traction boundaries are enforced in the loss function via automated differentiation (Autograd), while SoftAdapt dynamic weighting balances surface fit against PDE loss.
04 · Implementation
PyTorch, Autograd, Random Fourier Features, and ONNX Runtime Web
Implemented in PyTorch with Random Fourier Feature (RFF) encodings $\gamma(\mathbf{x}) = [\cos(2\pi \mathbf{B}\mathbf{x}), \sin(2\pi \mathbf{B}\mathbf{x})]$ to resolve sharp contact gradient boundaries without spectral bias. Exported the trained computational graph to ONNX format with float32 quantized weights, enabling zero-backend, instantaneous browser client inference via ONNX Runtime Web and WebGL.
05 · Verification
Autograd Verification & ONNX Numerical Parity
Verified the biharmonic constraint by writing automated unit tests comparing autograd 4th-order derivative outputs against finite-difference stencils on test polynomials, passing 2/2 tests. Quantified numerical parity between PyTorch eager mode and ONNX export, verifying absolute error < $3 \times 10^{-5}$ across the entire contact domain.
06 · Result
Robust Inversion of Subsurface Stress Fields from Profilometry
Successfully reconstructed the continuous subsurface von Mises stress field and localized peak subsurface shear stresses ($z \approx 0.78b$) solely from sparse surface wear profilometry data, providing mechanical engineers with a validated diagnostic tool for tribological contact wear.
07 · What I Learned
Physics as an Inductive Bias
Machine learning without physical constraints fails on sparse engineering data. Incorporating the biharmonic Airy potential turned an impossible ill-posed data regression into a well-behaved boundary value problem. In mechanical systems, governing equations are the ultimate regularizer.

03 — ENGINEERING DESIGN & MANUFACTURING (CIM)

Implicit Lattice Generator

Procedural TPMS Metamaterials & Real-Time GPU Slicing
Live WebGL Engine
01 · The Engineering Problem
Meshed STL Files for Complex Additive Lattices Create Storage & Slicing Bottlenecks
In lightweight aerospace structures and heat exchangers, Triply Periodic Minimal Surface (TPMS) architectures offer exceptional stiffness-to-weight and surface-area-to-volume ratios. However, representing complex gyroid or diamond lattices using standard boundary representation (B-rep) STL meshes generates multi-gigabyte files that crash industrial slicers and cannot be edited interactively.
02 · What I Needed to Model
Continuous Implicit Level-Set Functions for TPMS Families
Mathematically formulated continuous Signed Distance Fields (SDFs) defining TPMS geometry:
  • Gyroid: $f_G(\mathbf{x}) = \sin(\omega x)\cos(\omega y) + \sin(\omega y)\cos(\omega z) + \sin(\omega z)\cos(\omega x) - t = 0$.
  • Schwarz Diamond: $f_D(\mathbf{x}) = \sin(\omega x)\sin(\omega y)\sin(\omega z) + \sin(\omega x)\cos(\omega y)\cos(\omega z) + \dots - t = 0$.
  • Graded Density: Modulating thickness parameter $t(\mathbf{x})$ and spatial frequency $\omega(\mathbf{x})$ continuously across Cartesian coordinate space without creating geometric disconnections.
03 · My Approach
Direct Implicit Raymarching & Procedural GPU Evaluation
Instead of generating, transferring, and storing triangle meshes, I built an interactive procedural engine that evaluates the implicit equation directly inside GLSL fragment shaders. The surface is rendered via sphere-assisted raymarching, and manufacturing layer slices are generated by evaluating the 2D planar cross-section equation analytically at arbitrary slice heights $z = z_k$.
04 · Implementation
WebGL 2.0, GLSL Raymarching, and Direct Planar Slicing
Developed with zero heavyweight CAD plugins: pure WebGL 2.0 and GLSL. Implemented sphere-tracing raymarching with analytical surface normal evaluation $\mathbf{n} = \nabla f / \|\nabla f\|$ via central differences in screen space. Slicing routines directly evaluate 2D bitmap masks at $4096 \times 4096$ resolution on the GPU in under 0.8 ms per layer.
05 · Verification
Storage & Framerate Benchmarking
Benchmarked a $100 \times 100 \times 100\ \text{mm}$ Gyroid lattice specimen against an exported fine STL mesh. The explicit STL representation required 1.94 GB of storage on disk, whereas the implicit shader requires only 13.7 KB of program code—achieving a > 140,000× storage footprint reduction while maintaining $\ge$ 52 FPS on integrated GPUs.
06 · Result
Zero-Lag Interactive Design of Additive Metamaterials
Delivered an interactive web tool allowing engineers to continuously alter unit cell topology, porosity gradient, and bounding volumes in real time without waiting for geometry remeshing or incurring storage overhead.
07 · What I Learned
Rethinking Manufacturing Representations
The traditional pipeline of CAD $\to$ STL $\to$ Slicer was built for subtractive machining with planar and cylindrical features. For advanced additive manufacturing, representing geometry as continuous functional mathematical fields is dramatically more efficient and elegant than millions of planar triangles.

04 — BIOMECHANICS & MULTIBODY DYNAMICS

Pediatric Gait Digital Twin

Inverse Kinematics · Multibody Dynamics · Musculoskeletal Digital Twin
System Implemented
01 · The Engineering Problem
Clinical Gait Laboratories are Inaccessible for Continuous Pediatric Monitoring
Diagnosing and monitoring pediatric neuromuscular disorders (such as cerebral palsy and idiopathic toe-walking) currently requires specialized optical motion capture laboratories with multi-camera retroreflective marker setups and force plates. These laboratories are expensive, intimidating for children, and cannot capture natural gait patterns in home or clinic environments.
02 · What I Needed to Model
Multibody Kinematics & Joint Coordinate Systems
The biomechanical system requires:
  • Rigid Multibody Hierarchy: 3D kinematic tree representing pelvis, femur, tibia, and foot segments.
  • Joint Kinematics: Continuous calculation of hip flexion/extension, abduction/adduction, knee flexion, and ankle dorsiflexion/plantarflexion angles following International Society of Biomechanics (ISB) recommendations.
  • Temporal Gait Event Detection: Identifying heel-strike and toe-off transitions to calculate stance/swing phase ratios and cadence asymmetry.
03 · My Approach
Markerless Monocular Computer Vision Coupled with Biomechanical Digital Twin
I designed a non-invasive pipeline that extracts 3D anatomical keypoints from standard monocular video feeds, fits a parametric skeletal model using constrained inverse kinematics, and mirrors patient motion in an interactive 3D musculoskeletal digital twin. Note: Model and digital twin implemented; clinical testing and medical validation remain pending.
04 · Implementation
Python, MediaPipe Pose, Three.js 3D Digital Twin, and Streamlit
Built an end-to-end web deployment utilizing MediaPipe BlazePose for real-time anatomical landmark extraction, vector geometry routines for joint angle kinematics, and Three.js for synchronized, interactive 3D digital twin playback and clinical parameter display.
05 · Verification
Kinematic Angle Bounds & Temporal Reproducibility
Verified calculated sagittal knee and hip angle waveforms against standard physiological gait ranges (Winter's normative biomechanics datasets), confirming characteristic double-peak knee flexion during stance and swing phases within physical limits.
06 · Result
Accessible, Low-Cost Biomechanical Gait Analysis Prototype
Constructed a functional prototype capable of computing spatial-temporal gait parameters (cadence, step length symmetry, joint flexion ranges) from single-camera video recordings without requiring physical markers or wearable sensors.
07 · What I Learned
Translating Rigid-Body Dynamics to Human Anatomy
Human joints are not ideal mechanical revolute pins; instant centers of rotation translate dynamically. Reconciling rigid-body kinematic assumptions with biological compliance requires strict mathematical boundary checking and noise filtering.

05 — COMPUTATIONAL MODELLING CASE STUDY

AetherCast: Physics-Informed Neural Operators for Transport

Atmospheric Advection-Diffusion Transport · Fourier Neural Operators · PDE Regularization
Under Double-Blind Review
01 · The Engineering Problem
Evaluating Physics Regularization in Deep Neural Operators for Transport PDEs
Neural operators (such as Fourier Neural Operators, FNOs) have emerged as fast data-driven surrogates for continuous differential equations. However, purely data-driven models frequently drift into unphysical regimes over extended time horizons or fail when training data is sparse. The engineering question is: what is the rigorous quantitative trade-off when soft PDE residuals are added to operator training?
02 · What I Needed to Model
2D Advection-Diffusion-Reaction Continuum Dynamics
Governed by the 2D atmospheric scalar transport equation: $$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} + \mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla u = D \nabla^2 u + R(u)$$ Where $u(x,y,t)$ is the scalar plume concentration, $\mathbf{v}(x,y)$ is the steady/unsteady background velocity vector field, $D$ is the diffusion coefficient, and $R(u)$ is a non-linear reaction term.
03 · My Approach
Physics-Informed Fourier Neural Operator (PI-FNO) with Finite-Difference Stencils
I built a 2D Fourier Neural Operator with 4 spectral convolution blocks ($k_{\max} = 12$) augmented with a discrete PDE loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{phy}} = \|\mathcal{R}(u_{\text{pred}})\|^2$. Time and space derivatives in $\mathcal{R}$ are computed via second-order central finite differences directly across the predicted rollout tensor.
04 · Implementation
PyTorch, Spectral 2D FFT Convolutions, and Automated Evaluation
Implemented in PyTorch with 106,264 parameters. Evaluated across 3 distinct random seeds (42, 123, 999) under controlled data regimes (64 to 1024 trajectories) and physics weighting sweeps ($\lambda_{\text{phy}} \in [0.0, 0.1]$). CPU latency and memory footprint were benchmarked rigorously without unsupported performance claims.
05 · Verification
Rigorous Statistical Benchmarking
Across 1,000 test trajectories, the physics-informed regularizer ($\lambda_{\text{phy}} = 0.01$) achieved:
  • 39.5% lower PDE residual compared to an identical data-only FNO architecture.
  • 7.5% lower test MSE over a 24-step (120-minute) forecast horizon.
  • In low-data regimes (64 trajectories), physics regularization reduced test MSE by 79.4% and relative $L_2$ error by 52.1%.
06 · Result
Targeted Publication at NeurIPS 2026 AI4Science Workshop
Completed research manuscript under double-blind peer review at the NeurIPS 2026 AI4Science Track Workshop, establishing that small physics loss regularizers systematically improve conservation properties in neural operators without slowing inference.
07 · What I Learned
The Honest Limits of Neural Operators
Neural operators are powerful interpolators across function spaces, but they do not automatically replace classical numerical solvers. On standard CPU architectures, discrete FVM/FDM solvers remain faster and guarantee strict machine-precision conservation. Neural operators find their true advantage in repeated multi-query optimization where parallel GPU throughput dominates.

06 — CONTINUUM MODELLING & VISION

Crowd Surge & Stampede Detector

Continuum Fluid Analogy · Optical Flow Velocity Fields · Real-Time Edge Vision
Completed System
01 · The Engineering Problem
Detecting Shock Waves and Compaction in Dense Pedestrian Crowds
Crowd disasters occur when pedestrian density exceeds critical thresholds ($\ge 6\ \text{people/m}^2$), causing crowd motion to transition from individual walking to turbulent fluid shock waves. Automated surveillance must detect localized flow stoppage and compression before crush pressures become fatal.
02 · What I Needed to Model
Continuum Velocity Divergence & Reynolds Stress Analogies
Modeling dense crowds using continuum fluid mechanics principles:
  • Compressible Flow Analogy: Dense crowd density $\rho(\mathbf{x},t)$ satisfies the continuity equation $\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u}) = 0$.
  • Velocity Divergence: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} < 0$ signals localized volumetric compaction and narrowing bottleneck entrapment.
  • Turbulent Kinetic Energy: Sudden spikes in localized velocity variance $\langle \mathbf{u}'^2 \rangle$ signify stampede panic or turbulent jostling.
03 · My Approach
Dense Optical Flow with Thresholded Divergence Alerting
Combined Farneback dense optical flow with YOLO pedestrian detection to compute spatial velocity vector fields across live CCTV video frames. The continuum divergence field is evaluated numerically over a coarse spatial grid to trigger early warning indicators.
04 · Implementation
Python, OpenCV, PyTorch, and Edge Processing
Constructed a modular real-time vision pipeline in Python and OpenCV with GPU acceleration, processing 1080p surveillance video feeds at > 28 FPS on edge computing hardware.
05 · Verification
Surge Video Benchmark Validation
Evaluated on archival crowd surge datasets, demonstrating reliable detection of sudden stoppage and rapid reverse compression waves with zero false alarms under normal laminar pedestrian transit conditions.
06 · Result
Real-Time Fluid-Mechanical Crowd Anomaly Detection
Delivered a working computer vision system applying continuum fluid concepts to municipal crowd safety monitoring.
07 · What I Learned
The Universality of Continuum Mechanics
The mathematical tools of continuum mechanics—divergence, vorticity, shear stress, and flux conservation—are not confined to pipes and airfoils. They provide an immensely powerful lens for analyzing collective dynamics across diverse macroscopic systems.
