04 · Intellectual Map

Research Vision & Scientific Landscape

How can computation help us understand, simulate, and eventually design complex physical systems? This page charts my technical trajectory from foundation models to neural PDE operators, continuum solvers, and emerging inquiries in physical computing.

Narrative Arc

The Progression of My Research

01
Foundations · 2023–2025

Machine Learning & Representation Learning

Began in applied ML and low-resource NLP (transformers, few-shot classification, fine-tuning). Grounded my understanding of gradient optimization, latent embeddings, and statistical learning. Evidence: DravidianLanTech 2025 (ACL Anthology), FIRE 2023.

02
Bridging Mechanics · 2024–2025

Physics-Informed Machine Learning (PINNs)

Fused mechanical engineering training with deep learning. Explored how continuum physics (biharmonic Airy stress equilibrium, kinematic wear equations) can be directly encoded as differential operators into neural loss landscapes. Evidence: MicroWear-PINN (SoftAdapt, 2/2 autograd tests pass).

03
Operator Learning · 2025–2026

Neural Operators & Infinite-Dimensional PDE Mappings

Transitioned from coordinate networks to neural operators (FNOs) that map between infinite-dimensional function spaces. Formulated physics-regularized Fourier spectral convolutions to bound autoregressive rollout error in transient transport PDEs. Evidence: AetherCast (Under review at NeurIPS 2026 AI4Science Workshop).

04
High-Performance Simulation · 2025–2026

GPU-Accelerated Scientific Computing & Mechanics

Realized that building rigorous surrogate models requires deep fluency in forward numerical solvers. Implemented C++/CUDA GPU memory pipelines, cuSPARSE solvers, and finite-volume discretizations for coupled multiphysics topology optimization. Evidence: DynaMesh-Thermal (penalized Navier-Stokes/Darcy SIMP solver).

05
Geometric Representations · 2025–2026

Computational Geometry & Architected Structures

Shifted focus to compact representations of complex physical geometry. Investigated Signed Distance Fields (SDFs) and Triply Periodic Minimal Surfaces (TPMS) for zero-footprint metamaterial evaluation on GPUs. Evidence: Implicit Lattice (>140,000× measured storage reduction benchmark).

06
Emerging Research Horizon · 2027+

Metamaterials & Physical Information Systems

Synthesizing computational mechanics, neural representations, and geometry to explore physical computing: how matter itself can store, process, and dissipate information through architected structures, spin systems, and nonlinear mechanics. Developing toward: Prospective MRes / PhD research direction.
