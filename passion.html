05 · Curiosity & Human Perspective

Ideas, Instincts & Intellectual Rabbit Holes

Research is not just a ledger of code and citations; it is driven by what keeps someone staring into the void late into the night. Here is a glimpse into the questions, instincts, and conceptual obsessions behind my work.

Epistemology

I tend to learn things by trying to build them.

Reading an equation in a continuum mechanics textbook is informative, but deriving its discrete residuals, watching an optimizer stall because of stiff eigenvalues, and implementing a custom BiCGStab kernel in CUDA is where genuine intuition forms. Most of my projects began not as formal assignments, but as an attempt to make something computationally real from first principles.

Sonic Computation

Coding Music & Algorave: Algorithms as Sound.

I love the culture of livecoding and algorave — turning pure functional code into rhythmic, generative music in real time. Writing declarative polyrhythmic patterns in Haskell via TidalCycles, orchestrating sound synthesis within the Pulsar editor, and treating time as a continuous mathematical cycle. It is code stripped of corporate utility: computation expressed as pure texture and sonic architecture.

Subatomic Realm

Particle Physics, CERN & Fundamental Symmetries.

Deeply captivated by high-energy particle physics and experiments at CERN. The surreal reality of subatomic matter: ghost-like neutrinos that oscillate between flavors across planetary distances, fractional charges of quarks bound by gluons, second-generation muons raining down from cosmic-ray collisions, and gauge symmetry breaking. It represents the ultimate continuum where fields manifest as discrete particles.

Astrophysics & Cosmic Silence

Exoplanetary Worlds & Fermi's Paradox.

The staggering discovery of thousands of exoplanets orbiting distant stars — rogue worlds, lava oceans, tidally locked super-Earths — naturally collides with Fermi's Paradox: If the universe is billions of years old and planets are ubiquitous, where is everybody? Contemplating the Great Filter, biosignature detection via transit spectroscopy, and the vast acoustic silence of interstellar space keeps human problems in perspective.

The Core Fascination

Fascinated by invisible systems.

Much of what governs the universe is invisible until mapped mathematically: stress fields flowing through an elastic solid, concentration plumes transported by turbulent winds, latent manifolds learned by neural operators, neutrino fluxes penetrating light-years of lead, or magnetic spins configured in an energy landscape. Computation is the telescope that lets us visualize, query, and design these invisible structures.
