05 · Curiosity & Human Perspective

Ideas, Instincts & Intellectual Rabbit Holes

Research is not just a ledger of code and citations; it is driven by what keeps someone staring at a terminal late into the night. Here is a glimpse into the questions, instincts, and conceptual obsessions behind my work.

Epistemology

I tend to learn things by trying to build them.

Reading an equation in a continuum mechanics textbook is informative, but deriving its discrete residuals, watching an optimizer stall because of stiff eigenvalues, and implementing a custom BiCGStab kernel in CUDA is where genuine intuition forms. Most of my projects began not as formal assignments, but as an attempt to make something computationally real from first principles.

Interdisciplinary Instinct

I like questions that don't fit neatly inside one field.

Mechanical engineering gives me a visceral respect for physical conservation laws, stress equilibria, and thermodynamics. Machine learning provides flexible high-dimensional function approximation. Applied mathematics provides the language of operators and spectral spaces. The most fertile questions sit squarely at their intersections.

The Core Fascination

Fascinated by invisible systems.

Much of what governs the universe is invisible until mapped mathematically: stress fields flowing through an elastic solid, concentration plumes transported by turbulent winds, latent manifolds learned by neural operators, or magnetic spins configured in an energy landscape. Computation is the telescope that lets us visualize, query, and design these invisible structures.
